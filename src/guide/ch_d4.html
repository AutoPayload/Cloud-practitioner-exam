Pricing models and free tier

Must know Read the whole chapter.

AWS pricing follows a few simple rules: pay for what you use, pay less when you commit, and pay less when you use more. The exam checks that you can pick the cheapest option that still meets the requirement.

The pricing principles

  • Pay as you go: no upfront cost, no long-term contract.
  • Save when you commit: Reserved Instances (EC2, RDS, ElastiCache, Redshift, OpenSearch), DynamoDB reserved capacity, and Savings Plans.
  • Pay less by using more: tiered volume discounts, for example S3 storage and data transfer out.
  • Pay less as AWS grows: AWS lowers prices as its scale grows.

Savings Plans

PlanDiscountFlexibility
Compute Savings Plansup to 66%Any instance family, size, OS, tenancy and Region, and also Fargate and Lambda

You commit to a dollar amount per hour for 1 or 3 years. Usage above the commitment is billed On-Demand. Cost Explorer recommends a commitment for you.

Data transfer: data coming in to AWS is free. Traffic between instances in the same AZ over private IPs is free. Traffic between AZs, between Regions and out to the internet costs money.

Free to use (you pay only for the resources they create): IAM, VPC, CloudFormation, Elastic Beanstalk, Auto Scaling, AWS Organizations and consolidated billing.

Free tier and the Free Plan

  • Classic Free Tier (used in most exam questions) has three kinds of offers: Always Free (for example Lambda, 1 million requests a month), 12 months free for new accounts, and short free trials.
  • Free plan (new accounts since July 2025): credits replace the 12-months-free offers (Always Free still applies), and you pay nothing; the plan ends after 6 months or when the credits run out. The Paid plan charges you after the credits are used.

Accounts and governance

Must know Read the whole chapter.

Large companies run many AWS accounts, for example one per team or per environment. AWS Organizations groups them, gives one bill, and applies guardrails. Control Tower sets all of this up the right way.

AWS Organizations

  • One management account and many member accounts, grouped into organizational units (OUs) such as Dev, Prod or Finance.
  • Consolidated billing: one bill and one payment method. Usage is combined for volume discounts, and Reserved Instance and Savings Plans discounts are shared across accounts. It is free.
  • Service control policies (SCPs): guardrails that set the maximum permissions for accounts or OUs, for example “nobody may use services outside eu-west-1.” They apply to every user and role in a member account, including its root user. They don't apply to the management account, and they never grant permissions by themselves.

Related services

ServiceUse it to
AWS Control TowerSet up and govern a secure multi-account environment (a landing zone) in a few clicks, with preventive and detective controls and a compliance dashboard. It runs on top of Organizations.
AWS Resource Access Manager (RAM)Share resources (subnets, Transit Gateways, Route 53 Resolver rules, License Manager configurations) with other accounts instead of duplicating them.
AWS Service CatalogOffer users a catalog of pre-approved, correctly configured products (CloudFormation templates) they can launch themselves.
Tags and Resource GroupsLabel resources (Environment=Prod, Team=Data). Cost allocation tags show costs per tag in billing reports. Tag Editor manages tags in bulk.
AWS IAM Identity CenterOne sign-in for users across all accounts in the organization.

Why many accounts: separate billing per team, isolation between dev and prod, separate service quotas, regulatory boundaries, and a central account for logs (send all CloudTrail logs to one S3 bucket).

Don't confuse

SCP vs IAM policy. An IAM policy grants permissions to users and roles. An SCP only limits what an account can do; a user needs both an IAM Allow and no SCP block. Organizations vs Control Tower: Organizations is the engine; Control Tower is the automated setup with best-practice guardrails on top of it.

Cost management tools

Must know Read the whole chapter.

AWS gives you tools to estimate costs before you build, see where money goes, get alerts before you overspend, and find savings. The exam asks which tool fits each moment.

Estimate, track, control, optimize

Cost and Usage Report (Data Exports)Detailed analysisThe most detailed line-by-line billing data, delivered to S3 and queried with Athena, Redshift or QuickSight.

Service QuotasOperateSee your limits (quotas) and request increases; alarm when you get close.
AWS Billing ConductorRebillCustom pricing and billing views, for resellers or to bill each internal team for its own usage.

If the question saysThink

Don't confuse

Pricing Calculator vs Cost Explorer. The calculator estimates the future cost of something you haven't built. Cost Explorer analyzes what you already spent (and forecasts from it). Budgets vs billing alarm: Budgets can alert on forecasts and track usage and reservations; a billing alarm watches only the actual total.

Memory hook

Before: Calculator. After: Explorer. Guard rails: Budgets. Surprises: Anomaly Detection. Every detail: Cost and Usage Report.

New plan names (December 2025)

Basic stays free. Business Support+ replaces Developer and Business: 24/7 access to engineers and the full Trusted Advisor checks. Enterprise keeps the designated TAM and the 15-minute response. Unified Operations is the new top plan, with a team of specialists.
